# Pricing and free collaboration review — 9 October 2026

BigDaySorted recommendations below remain proposals. LiveDaysSorted now publishes the revised pre-launch festival/concert structure below, following the organiser’s decision. Neither product has working purchase enforcement or native cloud synchronisation yet.

## Current LiveDaysSorted structure — approved 9 October 2026

One festival edition receives a larger free contributing team for a higher fixed event price. Concert subscriptions support more simultaneously active concerts and fewer contributors per show. This is the current published pre-launch structure; it supersedes the earlier LiveDaysSorted pricing recommendations later in this review.

Festival passes include up to 14 event days and five stages within one edition, with 12 months from activation and no auto-renewal. No contributor personally pays to participate within the organiser's allowance; organiser seats are additional to free contributor numbers. Capacity is designed for per-event contribution and pooled account storage, not free cloud space for every invited user. Participation quotas, billing and native participant sync are still release work, not enabled by publishing this price table.

## Decision

The original Business package gives away too much *organiser capacity*: £19.99/month for 25 active events, five staff and 100 GB. The largest economic risk is support, maintenance and professional value being priced as a consumer utility; basic web requests alone are unlikely to require charging each guest. Keep participation free and make the organiser pay for authority, active events, participant capacity, storage and service duration. Sell dependable event delivery, not just invitations or a gallery.

BigDaySorted competes against free wedding tools as well as paid professional tools. LiveDaysSorted should command more for multi-day/multi-stage coordination, but it cannot claim the value of ticketing, payroll, accreditation, artist advancing or venue CRM until those exist. Both brands can share backend/permission/billing code, with brand-specific limits and prices. A cross-brand business bundle is a later experiment, not two automatically duplicated licences.

| Existing offer | Assessment | Change to test |

Storage is an account-level pool including galleries, documents and retained archives, not an allowance per invited user or per event. For recurring professional tiers, test 500 named guest/viewer identities per event on BigDaySorted and 1,000 programme/gallery identities on LiveDaysSorted; audience attendance is not the same as app users. Large public festival audiences require a cached read-only programme with separate usage planning, not an administrator invitation for every ticket holder.

The pass includes hosted editing without a Pro prerequisite. A monthly subscriber does not need Pro either. Pro buys independent local use; subscriptions/pass purchases buy time-limited hosted service. A price higher than the website trial demonstrates is a hypothesis until tested.

Pass expiry proposal: earlier of 18 months after activation and 90 days after the event, with the exact calendar expiry shown before purchase. Only sell the standard pass when this covers the event and intended post-event period; otherwise offer an explicitly priced extension (test £14.99 celebration / £29.99 live for six months) or delay activation. No promise of lifetime online storage. Avoid monthly personal subscriptions accidentally giving many months of free hosting after cancellation.

Yearly proposals give roughly two months free. Exact guest limits can be upgraded via capacity packs instead of blocking an event near its date. Test extra 25 helper/crew identities at £9.99 per active month or £24.99 per pass; extra 10 GB at £2.99/month or £14.99 per remaining pass term. These are hypotheses, not implemented add-ons. Exclude bulk marketing/SMS, long video hosting, unlimited personal technical support and custom integrations from base prices. Do not advertise paid tiers' unbuilt features.

## What free invited users should be able to do

| Role | Included contribution | Must not be able to do |
|---|---|---|
| Celebration guest | Own RSVP/preferences; own gift pledge; approved programme; bounded photo uploads | Other guests' personal data; confidential supplier finances; organiser administration. |
| Helper/crew | Assigned tasks/slots; confirm completion; relevant schedule/instructions; bounded documents/photos as allowed | Create independent events; change global timetable/dependencies/graph layout; invite others; administer billing; download whole private event archive. |
| Honouree/client | Approved client view/decisions; own response | Gift choices before reveal; hidden internal information. |
| Gallery/programme viewer | Approved read-only media/programme | Upload, edit or manage people. |
| Paid organiser/editor | Create events; assign work; manage participants; edit graph/timetable; budgets and moderation | Exceed contracted capacity or permissions on another organisation. |

An organiser can have a fixed entitlement including a co-organiser without selling that person a separate account subscription. Independent management rights consume the account's paid-editor allowance even when someone is labelled a helper. Tie all contributor storage, email and API usage to the event's paying account. Guests should not be billed individually; changing an invitation name must not change permissions.

Account possession, a downloaded APK/EXE, local flags or an imported .bigday file must not grant paid hosted organiser rights. Server-held entitlement and event membership decide access. Invitation links should be claimable by a verified individual, device-revocable and narrowly scoped. Do not distribute owner credentials or a complete owner archive to helpers. An imported local archive creates a new event only if the importing account is entitled to create it.

## Website/native architecture and current gaps

Use the same free-to-install application for organisers and participants. On first use offer Organise an event or Join an event. A join user can scan/open a personal link without buying Pro. It loads a role-filtered event view and submits narrow updates through the API. Cache only permitted data for offline viewing, with a visible last-updated time. Revoked/expired membership blocks new sync; offline previously downloaded information cannot be reliably recalled from a disconnected device. Event-day contributions need a queue/retry/conflict strategy; global schedule edits should remain organiser-only and online-authorised. Scheduled data access should renew, rather than granting an indefinite offline licence to a free contributor.

Current code has server-side assigned-task restrictions and browser invitation roles, but not a genuine native contributor/sync mode, commercial authentication/billing or entitlements. Helper API responses currently contain the full planning data; narrow their readable projection before inviting real suppliers/crew. Preview galleries currently permit JPEG/PNG/PDF only, 10 MB per upload, 100 MB per event—not the advertised proposed GB allowances. These limits need replacing with actual atomic account/event quotas, per-person caps and safe moderation rules at launch. No automatic email sending is currently active.

Both websites remain owner-private. A valid application invitation does not bypass the hosting access gate. A commercial pilot needs deliberately shared/public application entry points plus application-level authentication/permissions. This review has not changed site sharing, installed native billing or claimed that free participants can already use a released native contributor app.

## Unit economics: ordinary requests are not the main threat

Provider list prices, not a quotation for the current managed preview's hosting:
- Cloudflare Workers paid minimum $5/month, 10M requests and 30M CPU-ms included; excess $0.30/M requests and $0.02/M CPU-ms.
- D1 paid allowance: 25B rows read, 50M rows written/month; excess $0.001/M reads and $1/M writes; 5 GB database storage included then $0.75/GB-month. Bad unindexed scans and rewriting large plans can turn requests into many rows/CPU-ms.
- R2 Standard: $0.015/GB-month; $4.50/M Class A operations and $0.36/M Class B; internet egress free. Account-wide free allowance 10 GB, 1M A and 10M B. Do not award this free allowance anew to every customer.
- Backblaze B2 now quotes $6.95/TB/month, free egress up to 3x average stored data and $0.01/GB beyond, with qualifying partner routes including Cloudflare offering free egress. Not all direct download paths are free. KMT's B2 choice does not mean these previews already use B2; they currently bind R2.
- Resend Pro: $20/month for 50,000 transactional emails, then $0.90/1,000 at the quoted tier. The free tier's 100/day limit is inadequate for a 250-guest invitation burst.
- Stripe standard UK cards: 1.5% + £0.20; Stripe Billing pay-as-you-go adds 0.7% of Billing volume if used. International/premium cards, currency conversion, taxes on provider fees and optional services change this.

Model assumptions: £0.80 per USD (illustrative fixed scenario, not live FX), average 5 CPU-ms per dynamic request, each paid account has one event and 250 invitees. All gallery reads are conservatively counted at origin, and no video processing is included. Email pricing uses the quoted Pro overage rule, not an optimised higher-volume contract. Database use assumed within paid allowances; this requires indexing and measurement. Include retained data, previews and archives in the actual stored GB measure.

| Scenario per month | Worker/API requests | Total files | Email sends | Workers | R2 storage + operations | Email | Subtotal | Per paying account |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 accounts, modest use | 2.5M | 1 TB | 50,000 | £4.00 | £11.88 | £16.00 | £31.88 | £0.32 |
| 1,000 accounts, modest use | 25M | 10 TB | 500,000 | £9.12 | £123.48 | £340.00 | £472.60 | £0.47 |
| 1,000 accounts, heavy galleries | 100M | 20 TB | 500,000 | £33.12 | £265.80 | £340.00 | £638.92 | £0.64 |

Operation assumptions: 0.2M writes/1M reads in the first scenario; 2M/10M in the second; 5M/50M in the third. Modest request use corresponds to 100 requests per invited identity per month (including app opening/viewing actions), not 100 visits. These subtotals exclude D1 overages, provider taxes, backups, thumbnail generation, logs, authentication, monitoring, store/developer accounts, failed sends/retries, payment costs, labour, refunds, acquisition and development. Do not label them all-in operating costs or profit.

Heavy polling example: 250,000 participants polling every ten seconds for eight hours generates 720M requests before any gallery/normal usage. At the assumed 5 CPU-ms, Workers alone would be about $289.40 (about £231.52) on this direct list-price model; database/auth/log/notification work is additional. This is manageable but unnecessary, and at festival scale it increases sharply. Prefer foreground refresh, ETags/deltas, sensible intervals, pagination and event-day push/change notifications as needed. Cache public programme data, not private invitation responses. Client thumbnails/compression and lazy-loaded galleries are more useful than giant headline storage caps.

## Support and tax sensitivity

Assume VAT registration at 20%, UK Stripe card plus optional Billing fee (2.2% + £0.20 total in the model), files stored at full R2 quota, and support time costed at £30/hour. These are business-planning scenarios, not your current VAT status or tax advice. Revenue excluding VAT = price/1.2. Platform/store purchases require a separate margin model; Apple Small Business eligibility can provide a 15% rate, while Google Play's current regional/install/billing programmes differ. Do not assume web-Stripe fees for in-app purchases or that redirecting customers removes store obligations.

| Personal £9.99 / 10 GB | £8.33 | £0.42 | £0.12 | £7.79 | £0.29 |

| Business £49.99 / 50 GB | £41.66 | £1.30 | £0.60 | £39.76 | £32.26 |

At £19.99 for 25 simultaneously active events, net revenue before costs is only about 67p per active event/month under this VAT scenario. Two support contacts of 15 minutes each would consume more than the £14.82 remaining before infrastructure/marketing/development. The £9.99 personal tier must be largely self-service. Support the paying organiser; guests use contextual help and report event questions to their organiser. Charge separately for guided business onboarding/custom setup; do not promise emergency phone coverage during a festival for a £25 subscription.

Annual prepayment helps cash flow but discounts effective monthly revenue: £499.99/year averages £41.67/month gross, not £49.99. Customer lifetime value must use contribution after variable costs and support. Do not use storage-only margins as an acquisition budget. Measure churn/renewal, refunds, support minutes and annual-pass extensions; higher professional pricing only works if the product reliably saves valuable work.

## Launch experiments and safeguards

1. Test a celebration Event Pass at £39.99/£49.99/£59.99, and a live pass at £79.99/£99.99/£129.99 with the same clearly stated allowances per test. Interview purchasers after using a real scoped pilot; a hypothetical questionnaire is weaker evidence than a paid booking/deposit trial.
2. Test Business at £39.99/£49.99/£69.99 for 10 events and three editors, using a specific repeated workflow. Treat deposits/preorders only under agreed terms and a feasible release date; no payments are being collected by this review.
3. Track uploads/bytes, actual peak requests, API CPU, rows read/written, active contributors, support per organiser, conversion and refunds. Include both 100% quota and measured 25/50% usage cases.
4. Set account/event storage, participant, email, upload and active-event limits on the server. Add alerts and quotas before switching from private preview to real guests. Count free members against the organiser's allowance, without making them pay.
5. Give subscribers/pass owners clear expiry notices. When service ends, stop contributions and sync rather than quietly keeping costly hosted service alive. Provide a stated export/download window and deletion date before purchase; a tested retention policy is needed, not a hidden immediate deletion. Pro owners retain the purchased local application. Free participants do not inherit organiser rights. Rejoining a paid plan legitimately should not incur invented backdated charges.
6. Keep proposed prices explicitly labelled until billing, privacy, guest/native authentication, revocation and role filtering work. The app's current absence of payment enforcement must not be mistaken for a commercially sustainable free product.
